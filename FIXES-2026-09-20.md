# Nirmal Karaoke v20 — Spotify-inspired seamless LiveKit audio

## Core idea
Spotify uses buffered chunked delivery to protect continuous playback from short network stalls. LiveKit is a real-time WebRTC transport, so v20 applies the same principle without replacing LiveKit: high-priority stereo music + redundant audio packets + a larger receiver continuity cushion + audio-first video degradation.

## Changes
- Host AudioContext uses `latencyHint: playback` to prioritize sustained playback stability.
- System/screen audio bypasses the intermediate dynamics compressor and goes directly to the mix bus; the master limiter remains the final protection stage.
- Program audio stays stereo, DTX disabled, RED enabled, high RTP priority, 96 kbps.
- Audience receiver starts around 850 ms playout delay and grows adaptively up to 3.5 s on sustained loss/jitter/concealment.
- Stall/waiting events immediately increase continuity protection and retry playback.
- Buffer shrinks slowly after prolonged healthy telemetry instead of oscillating.
- WebRTC inbound-rtp concealment telemetry is included in the controller.
- Under severe congestion, non-admin audience video subscriptions are temporarily shed to protect audio. They are restored after a sustained healthy period.
- `webAudioMix: false` is explicit on the audience room; the host program remains a normal LiveKit remote audio track attached directly for playback.

## Limitation
This cannot reproduce Spotify's HTTP/CDN chunking exactly because a LiveKit room is a real-time WebRTC session. Very poor networks can still have limits; the design intentionally trades some live latency for continuity.
